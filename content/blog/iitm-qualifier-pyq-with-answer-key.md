## What a qualifier paper looks like

Each course paper uses three question formats:

- **MCQ**: one correct option.
- **MSQ**: one or more correct options. Treat each option as its own true/false question.
- **NAT**: type a number. Check the rounding asked for.

Here is the format mix in the papers on BTechi:

| Course | Questions per paper | Most common format | Typical style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maths I | 10-14 | NAT and MSQ | Short calculations, several sub-questions on one passage |
| Stats I | 13-18 | NAT and MSQ | One data set, then 2-3 questions on it |
| CT | 12-14 | MCQ | Read pseudocode on a dataset; say what a variable holds |
| English I | 35-40 | MCQ | Many 1-mark questions; passages, matching, conversations |

A pattern to notice: many questions come in **groups**. One passage or data set, then two or three questions on it. If you misread the passage, you lose the whole group. Read slowly.

## Four solved questions from real qualifier papers

Try each one before you read the answer. The answers match the official keys.

### 1. Maths I, May 2024: three sets, "exactly one"

**Question.** 500 candidates applied for a data analyst post. 285 know Python, 195 know C, 115 know Java. 45 know Python and Java, 70 know C and Python, 50 know C and Java. 50 know none of the three. How many know exactly one language?

**Solution.**

1. At least one language: 500 − 50 = 450.
2. Inclusion-exclusion: 450 = 285 + 195 + 115 − 45 − 70 − 50 + (all three). So 450 = 430 + (all three), and all three = **20**.
3. Exactly one = (sum of singles) − 2 × (sum of pairs) + 3 × (all three) = 595 − 2 × 165 + 3 × 20 = 595 − 330 + 60 = **325**.

The official key is 325. Find this question in the [Maths I May 2024 qualifier paper](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/qualifier-may-2024).

### 2. Maths I, January 2024: the grocery store

**Question.** 60 customers each bought at least one of bread, milk and fruits. 28 bought bread, 37 milk, 30 fruits. 16 bought bread and fruits, 17 bread and milk, and 9 bought all three. (a) How many bought milk and fruits? (b) How many bought milk and fruits but not bread?

**Solution.**

- (a) 60 = 28 + 37 + 30 − 16 − 17 − (milk and fruits) + 9. So 60 = 71 − (milk and fruits), and milk and fruits = **11**.
- (b) Milk and fruits but not bread = 11 − 9 = **2**.

Official keys: 11 and 2. Both are in the [Maths I January 2024 qualifier paper](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/qualifier-january-2024).

### 3. Stats I, May 2024: add 5 to every mark

**Question.** Manoj scored 60, 70, 65, 75, 80. Nitin scored 5 marks more than Manoj in each subject. (a) Find Nitin's mean. (b) Find the correlation coefficient between their marks.

**Solution.**

- (a) Manoj's mean = 350 / 5 = 70. Adding 5 to every value adds 5 to the mean, so Nitin's mean = **75**.
- (b) Nitin = Manoj + 5 is a straight line with a positive slope, so r = **1**.

Official keys: 75 and 1. Adding a constant moves the mean, but it does not change the spread or the correlation. This "shift or scale every value" idea is one of the most repeated Stats I question types on BTechi. See the [Stats I May 2024 qualifier paper](/pyqs/foundation/statistics-for-data-science-1/qualifier-may-2024).

### 4. English I, May 2024: collocations and a phone call

**Question (collocations).** Match Column A (Bank, Strong, Lazy, Short, Ancient) with Column B (Temper, Account, Ruins, Afternoon, Coffee).

**Answer.** Bank account, strong coffee, lazy afternoon, short temper, ancient ruins.

**Question (telephone).** Jay calls: "Hello? (i) ____" Prateek: "Hello. Yes, I am Prateek Agarwal." Which fits blank (i)?

**Answer.** "Am I talking to Prateek Agarwal?" The other options ask about a house, a family or a son, and none of them fit Prateek's reply.

Both sets are five 1-mark questions each. That is 10 marks from two short tasks. Practise them in the [English I May 2024 qualifier paper](/pyqs/foundation/english-1/qualifier-may-2024).

## How to sit qualifier PYQs like the real exam

Past papers only help if you use them in the right order. Here is a plan for the four weeks of the qualifier.


### Weeks 1-3: topic sheets, untimed

After each week of lectures, open the topic sheets for that week and solve them on paper. Do not time yourself. The goal is to learn the question types. You can also use [Maths I qualifier practice](/practice?course=mathematics-for-data-science-1&exam=qualifier), [Stats I qualifier practice](/practice?course=statistics-for-data-science-1&exam=qualifier), [CT qualifier practice](/practice?course=computational-thinking&exam=qualifier) and [English I qualifier practice](/practice?course=english-1&exam=qualifier) for short mixed drills.

### End of week 3: one course paper at a time, timed

Pick the oldest papers first (January 2023, May 2023). Sit one course paper at a time in the portal. Score it **by course** and write down every wrong answer in a mistakes log.

### Week 4: full 4-hour exams

Sit at least two full exams under real conditions: four hours, one sitting, no phone, no notes. Use the [May 2024 full exam](/qualifier/qualifier-may-2024) and the [January 2024 full exam](/qualifier/qualifier-january-2024). If you have already seen those questions, use [Mock 1](/qualifier/mock-1) and [Mock 2](/qualifier/mock-2) instead.

A simple time plan for the 4 hours:

| Order | Course | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your strongest course | 45 min |
| 2 | Your weakest course | 70 min |
| 3 | Second weakest | 55 min |
| 4 | Remaining course | 45 min |
| End | Review flagged questions | 25 min |

Do not leave your weakest course for the end. The 40% per-course rule means your weakest course decides your result.

### Last two days: repeats and mistakes only

No new papers. Read the most-repeated sheets and your mistakes log. Then read the [cheat notes for all four courses](/qualifier/notes): [Maths I](/qualifier/notes/maths-1), [Stats I](/qualifier/notes/stats-1), [CT](/qualifier/notes/ct) and [English I](/qualifier/notes/english-1).

> [!WARNING]
> Score every mock by course, not just overall. A 65% average with CT at 38% is a fail for the General category. A 54% average with every course above 40% is a pass.

> [!TIP]
> In each course, do two passes. First pass: answer everything you can do in under three minutes, and flag the rest. Second pass: go back to the flagged ones. This stops one hard question from eating your time.

## Mistakes people make with qualifier PYQs

- **Doing only the newest paper.** The 2023 papers are just as useful. Many question types repeat across all five sittings.
- **Skipping CT papers.** CT looks easy, but it needs exact tracing. Do every CT paper on paper, with a variable table.
- **Ignoring English until week 4.** English I papers have 35-40 questions. Sounds and idioms need daily practice.
- **Reading answers before trying.** Attempt first, then check the key.
- **Re-sitting the same paper and counting the score.** A second attempt measures memory, not skill. Use the mocks for a clean score.

All 18 course papers, plus at least two full 4-hour exams or mocks in week 4. That is about 15-20 hours of practice spread over four weeks.

### Is there negative marking in the qualifier exam?

Check the instructions in your exam and on your hall ticket. The marking scheme is stated there. We do not assume one.
