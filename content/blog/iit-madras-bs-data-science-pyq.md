## How IITM BS exams are organised

The IIT Madras BS programme runs in **three terms a year**: January, May and September. The official programme pages at [study.iitm.ac.in](https://study.iitm.ac.in/ds/) describe the structure. Each course has weekly online assignments, plus in-person exams. The exam papers you will find as PYQs are of four kinds.

| Exam | When | What it covers | Formats you will see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualifier | Before you join | Weeks 1-4 of Maths I, Stats I, CT and English I | MCQ, MSQ, NAT |
| Quiz 1 | Around the middle of a term | The first weeks of the course | MCQ, MSQ, NAT |
| Quiz 2 | Later in the term | The middle weeks of the course | MCQ, MSQ, NAT |
| End term | End of the term | The whole course | MCQ, MSQ, NAT, sometimes short answers |

The three question formats are the same across the programme:

- **MCQ**: exactly one option is correct.
- **MSQ**: one or more options are correct. Judge each option on its own.
- **NAT**: no options. You type a number.

> [!NOTE]
> The exact weight of each quiz and the end term in your final grade is set per course. Check the course page and the grading rules on [study.iitm.ac.in](https://study.iitm.ac.in/ds/academics.html) for your term. We do not guess weights here.

## The best way to use IITM BS PYQs

Most students waste past papers. They read questions and answers, feel good, and remember nothing. Here is a better plan, in three phases.

### Phase 1: during the term (weeks 1-8), go by topic

Do not start with full papers. After each week of lectures, open the **topic sheet** for what you just studied. Solve the questions on paper, then check the key.

- After Maths I week 1, do the [sets and Venn diagram questions](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/sheets/topic-sets-and-venn-diagrams).
- After Stats I week 2, do the [describing categorical data questions](/pyqs/foundation/statistics-for-data-science-1/sheets/topic-describing-categorical-data).
- After English I grammar weeks, do the [articles and prepositions questions](/pyqs/foundation/english-1/sheets/topic-prepositions-conjunctions-articles).

This tells you if you learned the week while there is still time to fix it.

### Phase 2: one week before a quiz, use the exam papers

Before Quiz 1, sit the Quiz 1 paper for your course in the timed portal, for example the [Maths I Quiz 1 October 2025 paper](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/quiz-1-oct-2025-afternoon). Before Quiz 2, sit the [Maths I Quiz 2 August 2025 paper](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/quiz-2-aug-2025-afternoon). Score it, then spend the next two days only on the questions you got wrong.

### Phase 3: two weeks before the end term, go full length

Sit every end-term paper for your course under real time, one per day, with a gap day to review. For Maths I that is four papers, from [December 2024](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/end-term-dec-2024-forenoon), [April 2025 (forenoon)](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/end-term-apr-2025-forenoon), [April 2025 (afternoon)](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/end-term-apr-2025-afternoon) and [August 2025](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/end-term-aug-2025-forenoon). On the last two days, read only the most-repeated sheet and your mistakes log.

> [!TIP]
> Keep a **mistakes log**: one line per wrong answer, with the reason (concept, careless, misread, time). Reading it the night before the exam is worth more than one more new paper.

### A real example: why topic practice works

Here is a question from the [Maths I September 2023 qualifier](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/qualifier-september-2023).

**Question.** In a survey of 140 people, 75% like cricket and 50% like football. What is the minimum number of people who like both games?

- Cricket = 0.75 × 140 = 105. Football = 0.50 × 140 = 70.
- The overlap is smallest when nobody is outside both groups. Then both = 105 + 70 − 140 = **35** (official key: 35).

Now look at the [Maths I May 2024 qualifier](/pyqs/foundation/mathematics-for-data-science-1/qualifier-may-2024): 500 job applicants, three programming languages, "how many know exactly one?" Same idea, three sets instead of two. The answer is 325 by inclusion-exclusion. Once you have solved a few of these on the topic sheet, the next one takes two minutes.

### A real example from Stats I

From the [Stats I January 2024 qualifier](/pyqs/foundation/statistics-for-data-science-1/qualifier-january-2024): ten students rate education as Fair, Fair, Excellent, Poor, Fair, Excellent, Poor, Poor, Excellent, Poor. What is the mode, and what is the median?

- Counts: Poor 4, Fair 3, Excellent 3. Mode = **Poor**.
- The data is ordinal, so order it: Poor ×4, Fair ×3, Excellent ×3. The 5th and 6th values are both Fair. Median = **Fair**.

The official key agrees on both. "Mode and median of ordinal data" is one of the most repeated Stats I question types on BTechi (4 times across all past papers).

## Common mistakes with PYQs

- **Only reading solutions.** You must attempt first. Reading a solution feels like learning, but it is not.
- **Using only the newest paper.** Old papers repeat. A 2023 qualifier question can teach you a 2025 quiz question.
- **Skipping the second session.** If an end term had forenoon and afternoon papers, do both.
- **Scoring overall, not by topic.** "62%" tells you nothing. "Quadratics 3/8" tells you what to study tomorrow.
- **Ignoring formats.** In MSQ, every option is its own true/false question. In NAT, check the rounding.
