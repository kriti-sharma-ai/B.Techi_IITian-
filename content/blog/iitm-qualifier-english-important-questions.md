## Why English I needs real preparation

The qualifier tests four courses: Maths I, Stats I, Computational Thinking and English I. A General-category candidate needs **at least 40% in every course and at least 50% on average** ([official admissions page](https://study.iitm.ac.in/ds/admissions.html)). So English is not a "free" course. A low English score can fail you even if your Maths is excellent.

Many people skip English because they speak it well. Then they meet questions on diphthongs, semi-vowels and separable phrasal verbs, which school never taught. The good news: these question types repeat a lot, so a few weeks of focused practice is enough.

The English I qualifier syllabus is the first four weeks of the course ([official course page](https://study.iitm.ac.in/ds/course_pages/BSHS1001.html)):

- Sounds and pronunciation
- Parts of speech and grammar
- Vocabulary and usage
- Reading comprehension

> [!NOTE]
> The real English paper also has listening questions with audio clips. The official papers do not include the audio, so the papers on BTechi leave those questions out. That is why each paper starts at Q16 or Q18.

## Solved IITM qualifier English questions

Every answer below matches the official answer key in the paper data.

### 1. Telephone conversation (May 2023)

> Harilal: Hello! Is this Nirjeevani Hospital?
>
> Receptionist: Yes. (i) ______
>
> Harilal: I wanted to know if Dr. Arundhati will be available for consultation.
>
> Receptionist: Yes Ma'am. (ii) ______ book an appointment?
>
> Receptionist: Please tell me your name and age. Hello! (iii) ______ *(the call gets disconnected)*
>
> Harilal: The call got (iv) ______. My name is Harilal Khan and I am 35 years old.
>
> Receptionist: Could you please (v) ______

| Blank | Options | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) | How may I help you today? / Who's calling, please? / Hello! Are you Narsimh? | **How may I help you today?** | A receptionist offers help first |
| (ii) | Are we to / Would you like to / Will you | **Would you like to** | The polite form for an offer |
| (iii) | Please ring me later. / Are you there? / I will talk to you later. | **Are you there?** | The line went quiet, so you check |
| (iv) | Put off / Called off / Cut off | **Cut off** | A call that drops is "cut off" |
| (v) | Speak up / Speak out / Call back | **Speak up** | Asking someone to talk louder |

The method: read the line **after** the blank. In (v), Harilal simply repeats his name and age, so the receptionist did not hear him. "Speak up" fits; "speak out" means to say an opinion in public.

### 2. Collocation matching (May 2024)

Match Column A with Column B: Bank, Strong, Lazy, Short, Ancient with Temper, Account, Ruins, Afternoon, Coffee.

- Bank → **account**
- Strong → **coffee**
- Lazy → **afternoon**
- Short → **temper**
- Ancient → **ruins**

Earlier papers used the same format. May 2023: land **acquisition**, moral **decay**, final **blow**, weather **forecast**, unanimous **decision**. September 2023: sports **equipment**, library **card**, garden **hose**, job **offer**, fishing **rod**.

Match in two passes. First lock the pairs you are sure about. Then the last one or two fall into place.

### 3. Synonym matching (January 2024)

Match: Prove, Maladroit, Tired, Coach, Coarse with Wearied, Counsel, Gravelly, Establish, Clumsy.

- Prove → **establish**
- Maladroit → **clumsy**
- Tired → **wearied**
- Coach → **counsel**
- Coarse → **gravelly**

"Maladroit" is the hard word. If you do not know it, solve the other four first. Only "clumsy" is left.

### 4. Diphthongs and semi-vowels (2023 and 2024)

A diphthong is a vowel sound that glides from one sound to another inside one syllable, like "oy" in *boy* or "ow" in *shout*.

- **Which word has no diphthong: Snout, Stout, Sport, Shout?** Answer: **Sport**. This exact question appeared in both September 2023 and January 2024.
- **Mouth, South, Pout, Moot?** Answer: **Moot** (May 2024). It has a long "oo", not a glide.
- **Loiter, Stew, Maiden?** Answer: **Stew** (May 2023).
- **Bottle, Boycott, Bail?** Answer: **Both Boycott and Bail** have a diphthong (May 2023).

Semi-vowel questions ask which glide sound joins two words when you speak them together. It appeared in three papers:

| Phrase | Semi-vowel | Paper |
|---|---|---|
| go out | /w/ | September 2023 |
| she offered | /y/ | January 2024 |
| me off | /y/ | May 2024 |

The rule: after a lip-rounded "oo" or "oh" sound (go, who), you hear /w/. After an "ee" sound (she, me), you hear /y/.

### 5. Odd one out by sound

These questions are about **sounds**, not spelling.

- **near, dear, tear, gear** → **gear** (May 2024). N, d and t are made with the tongue behind the teeth; g is made at the back of the mouth.
- **noun, down, town, gown** → **gown** (January 2024), for the same reason.
- **peat, beat, clear, pear** → **clear** (May 2024). P and b are made with the lips; the "cl" sound is made at the back.
- **knit, pit, kit, neat** → **neat** (September 2023). Here the vowel is the clue: knit, pit and kit have the short "i"; neat has the long "ee". The silent k in "knit" is the trap.
- **lit, mitt, knit, feet** → **feet** (January 2024), again for the long "ee".

Practise all five types with the [vowels and diphthongs topic sheet](/pyqs/foundation/english-1/sheets/topic-vowels-diphthongs-semivowels).

### 6. Articles, prepositions and agreement

| Question | Answer | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| There is ___ emergency exit to your left. (Jan 2024) | **an** | Vowel sound |
| This is ___ best chocolate cake I've ever had. (Jan 2024) | **the** | Superlatives take "the" |
| ___ Sun rises in the east. (May 2024) | **The** | Only one exists |
| They usually spend their holidays in ___ mountains. (Sep 2023) | **the** | Mountain ranges and "the mountains" |
| I want to buy ___ laptop computer next week. (May 2023) | **a** | One of many, consonant sound |
| She has not read anything ___ Monday. (May 2023) | **since** | A point in time with the present perfect |
| Sushma is worried ___ the exam. (Jan 2024) | **about** | "Worried about" is a fixed pair |
| The teacher, as well as the students, ___ responsible. (May 2023) | **is** | The subject is "the teacher"; "as well as" does not make it plural |

### 7. Phrasal verbs and idioms

A phrasal verb is **separable** if you can put the object in the middle. Test it with "it":

- **Sum up** → "sum it up" works, so it is separable. It was the answer in January 2024 (other options: live on, see about, take after).
- **Call by** → separable. It was the answer in May 2024 (other options: hit on, keep at, stand for).
- **Come across** → "come it across" is wrong, so it is inseparable (May 2023).

Idioms that have been asked in the qualifier: *a dark horse* (an unexpected winner), *cut corners* (do something the fastest and cheapest way), *break the ice* (remove the tension at a first meeting), *get out of hand* (become difficult to control), *cost an arm and a leg* (be very expensive), *keep someone in the loop* (keep them updated). Collect more from the [phrasal verbs and idioms topic sheet](/pyqs/foundation/english-1/sheets/topic-phrasal-verbs-idioms).

### 8. Reading comprehension (May 2023)

The May 2023 passage is about happiness. One line says: "Simple living encourages high thinking. It leads to contentment. Contentment gives us inner wealth."

**Which is the correct chain leading to happiness?** Answer: **simple living, high thinking, contentment, inner wealth**. Every wrong option uses the same four words in a different order. So underline the linking words (encourages, leads to, gives) and follow them.

**Which statement is NOT TRUE?** The options include "Renunciation is the result of perfect happiness." The passage says the **secret** of perfect happiness lies in renunciation. So renunciation is the cause, not the result. This option is the answer.

Reading questions are 2 marks each, so they deserve your calmest time. Read the questions first, then the passage, and mark where each answer is. Practise with the [reading comprehension topic sheet](/pyqs/foundation/english-1/sheets/topic-reading-comprehension).

## Common traps in English I

- **Spelling instead of sound.** "Knit" starts with an n sound. "Hour" starts with a vowel sound, so it is "an hour".
- **Look-alike words.** *Weak/week*, *weather/wether/whether*, *effected/affected*, *to/two/too* have all been asked. A *wether* is a male sheep, which is why "The ___ was unsold in the auction" takes **wether** (September 2023).
- **Abstract or collective?** "Parliament" is not an abstract noun (May 2023). It is a group of people.
- **Multiple-select parts of speech.** "Rinku reads quite clearly": both **quite** and **clearly** are adverbs (September 2023). "Shraddha is a brilliant student": both **Shraddha** and **student** are nouns (January 2024).
- **Your opinion versus the passage.** In reading questions, the answer must come from the text, even if you disagree.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not leave English to the last week. Sounds and idioms need short daily practice. Fifteen minutes a day for four weeks beats five hours the night before.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the most important questions for the IITM qualifier English exam?

Reading comprehension, the five-blank telephone conversation, five-item collocation or synonym matching, vowel and diphthong questions, and short grammar items on articles, prepositions and parts of speech. In the four qualifier papers, these blocks cover almost every mark.

### Where can I download the IITM qualifier English important questions PDF?

On BTechi, for free. Open any [English I question sheet](/pyqs/foundation/english-1/sheets), click **Save PDF** (or press P) and choose **Save as PDF**. For the important questions only, save the [English I most repeated questions sheet](/pyqs/foundation/english-1/sheets/most-repeated) as a PDF.

### Do English qualifier questions repeat?

Yes. The question "Which word has no diphthong: Snout, Stout, Sport, Shout?" appeared in both September 2023 and January 2024. Semi-vowel questions appeared in three papers, and every paper has a telephone conversation and a matching question.

### How many questions are in the English I qualifier paper?

The papers on BTechi have 35 to 40 questions worth 40 or 50 marks. These counts leave out the listening questions, because the official papers do not include the audio.

### Is English I hard in the IITM BS qualifier?

Most questions are short and 1 mark each. The hard parts are phonetics (sounds) and idioms, which most schools do not teach. With 15 to 20 minutes of daily practice for four weeks, most students can clear 40% comfortably.

### How should I prepare for reading comprehension in the qualifier?

Read the questions first, then the passage. Mark where each answer is in the text. Watch for options that use the right words in the wrong order, and for options that swap cause and effect.

### What is the cut-off for English in the IITM qualifier?

For the General category, you need at least 40% in English I and at least 50% average across all four courses. OBC-NCL and EWS candidates need 35% and 45%; SC, ST and PwD candidates need 30% and 40%. Check the official [admissions page](https://study.iitm.ac.in/ds/admissions.html) for your batch.
